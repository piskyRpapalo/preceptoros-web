PreceptorOS

The preceptor that lives on your machine. ES · FR

Your AI. On your computer. No cloud. Type below: if your browser can handle it, the model downloads once and from then on your own machine answers — no server, no account, no queue. And if it can't, you're not stranded: we hand you the text, ready to paste into the AI you already use.

This page hasn't asked anyone for anything yet: not a script, not a font, not a pixel from elsewhere. Type, and it will tell you what your browser can do — deciding is up to you.

What happens when you use it

  1. You type, and nothing leaves.

    The question stays in the tab. If your browser has WebGPU, the model runs inside it; the only moment anything crosses the network is the download you authorise, once.

  2. You install it for real.

    PreceptorOS comes down from the browser onto your computer and talks to the model through a pipe, not a port: McIlroy's rule, that each program do one thing well and that they speak through a common stream. That is why its voice can be swapped without rewriting the rest.

  3. Your memory is a file.

    SQLite, public domain, with its authors' commitment to keep the file format readable into the middle of this century. Your memory is not a row in someone else's database: it is a file you can copy, open and take with you.

  4. You count your own tokens.

    Your instance keeps track of what it spends. The day you want to know, the number will look like this: 50,000 tokens from your own machine, 0 from the cloud.

What this page does, and what it costs

  • It doesn't follow you. No cookies, no analytics, no identifier. The only thing kept in your browser is two letters (en) so it won't ask your language twice. The language lives in the path, so the metric comes from the URL and not from you.
  • On load it asks nobody for anything. No outside fonts and no outside scripts: everything you see came from this domain.
  • Four routes, and you get offered the most sovereign one you can run. If your browser already ships a model (Prompt API), that one: zero network. If it can but must fetch it, we tell you it is 2.7–4 GB before you press, even though that is more than ours. Otherwise WebLLM (~945 MB). And failing all that, the text for your AI. Never the other way round.
  • What downloading the model costs. If you press the button, your browser contacts esm.run (the WebLLM library) and huggingface.co (the weights). Those two will see your IP, the way any site you visit does. Until you press it, they won't. That is why the button never presses itself.
  • The four copy buttons copy the same thing. Exactly the same text. They are separate because you look for the name of your tool, not because the content changes. Telling you costs less than faking four things.
  • The browser model is small. It fits in a tab, and it shows: it is there to prove the idea, not to replace the one you install.
  • The face is art that already existed. The eight frames of the bust come from the Aurelius sprites, cropped and shrunk — not drawn here.
